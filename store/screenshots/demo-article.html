You Won't Believe What Riverton Just Did to E-Bike Owners

By Staff Writer · 6 min read

In a move that has left the entire city absolutely stunned, Riverton's council has unleashed a shocking new crackdown that could change everything for the thousands of residents who ride to work every day. Nobody saw this coming, and what happens next may surprise you.

Before we get into it, it is worth remembering how much e-bikes have changed the way we all get around, and how many of us have strong feelings about them, one way or another, for better or for worse.

What actually changes on 1 March

From 1 March, every e-bike battery sold or charged in shared residential buildings must carry a certified safety label (EN 50604 or UL 2849). Batteries without a label can still be used, but not charged in hallways, stairwells or shared storage rooms.

Fines start at 150 for a first offence and are issued to the owner, not the building. Landlords must post the rule in shared areas by 15 February.

How to check your battery in two minutes

Look for the certification mark on the battery casing or its charger, usually next to the voltage rating. If there is none, the manufacturer's site or receipt will list it. Batteries bought from major brands after 2023 almost always comply.

If yours is not certified, the city's trade-in scheme pays 40% of a certified replacement for residents, up to 200, until 31 May. Apply online with a photo of the old battery and proof of address.

Celebrities weigh in

Local influencer and reality star Danny Vex posted a tearful video calling the rule "a war on freedom", racking up two million views overnight. His ex-partner reportedly liked a post disagreeing with him, reigniting their very public feud.

Could this be the end of e-bikes?

Some experts fear this could be just the beginning. Will scooters be next? Will the city ban bikes from parks entirely by next summer? Nobody knows for sure, but insiders whisper that bigger changes may already be in the works.

Why the council acted

Fire service data presented to the council shows 41 battery fires in residential buildings last year, 29 of them during charging in shared spaces. Certified batteries were involved in 3.
